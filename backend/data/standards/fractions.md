# Georgia Standards of Excellence (MGSE) — Fractions

## Grade 5
### MGSE5.NF.1
Add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers with unlike denominators by finding a common denominator and replacing them with equivalent fractions.
- Summary: When denominators are different, find the least common denominator, convert both fractions so their bottom numbers match, and then add or subtract only the numerators while keeping the denominator identical.
- Key concepts: common denominator, equivalent fractions, LCD, simplifying to simplest form.
- Formula/Rule: a/b + c/d = (a*d + b*c) / (b*d).

### MGSE5.NF.4
Multiply a fraction by a whole number or another fraction.
- Summary: To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators straight across to get the new numerator, and multiply the denominators straight across to get the new denominator.
- Key concepts: multiplying straight across, fraction of a whole, area model tiling with fractional unit squares.
- Formula/Rule: (a/b) * (c/d) = (a*c) / (b*d).

### MGSE5.NF.7
Divide unit fractions by whole numbers and divide whole numbers by unit fractions.
- Summary: Understand division as sharing or grouping. Dividing a unit fraction by a whole number splits a small part into even smaller equal slices; dividing a whole number by a unit fraction counts how many fractional pieces fit inside the whole.
- Key concepts: unit fractions (1/b), partition into equal shares, visual fraction models.
- Formula/Rule: (1/b) / n = 1/(b*n); n / (1/b) = n * b.

## Grade 6
### MGSE6.NS.1
Interpret and compute quotients of fractions divided by fractions, and solve real-world word problems involving fraction division.
- Summary: Divide any fraction by another fraction using the reciprocal of the divisor: Keep the first fraction, Change division to multiplication, and Flip the second fraction (Keep-Change-Flip).
- Key concepts: reciprocal, multiplicative inverse, Keep-Change-Flip, visual tape diagrams.
- Formula/Rule: (a/b) / (c/d) = (a/b) * (d/c) = (a*d) / (b*c).

### MGSE6.NS.4
Find the greatest common factor (GCF) of two numbers up to 100 and the least common multiple (LCM) of two numbers up to 12.
- Summary: Use prime factorization or listing multiples to find common multiples when adding fractions, and greatest common factors when simplifying fractions to lowest terms.
- Key concepts: GCF for simplifying fractions, LCM for least common denominators.
