# Georgia Standards of Excellence (MGSE) — Geometry & Measurement

## Grade 5
### MGSE5.MD.1
Convert among different-sized standard measurement units within a given measurement system (metric and customary) and use these conversions in solving multi-step real-world problems.
- Summary: Convert between units such as meters to centimeters or feet to inches. When converting to a smaller unit, multiply by the conversion factor; when converting to a larger unit, divide.
- Key concepts: metric conversions, customary units, multi-step measurement problems.

### MGSE5.MD.5
Relate volume to the operations of multiplication and addition and solve real-world problems involving volume of right rectangular prisms.
- Summary: Understand that volume measures 3D space filled with unit cubes. Calculate volume by multiplying length by width by height (or base area times height). Solve perimeter (P = 2l + 2w) and area (A = l * w) as foundations.
- Key concepts: volume of rectangular prisms, unit cubes, base area, perimeter and 2D area formulas.
- Formula/Rule: Volume = length * width * height = Base * height; Perimeter = 2*(l + w); Area = l * w.

## Grade 6
### MGSE6.G.1
Find the area of right triangles, other triangles, special quadrilaterals, and polygons by composing into rectangles or decomposing into triangles.
- Summary: Any triangle has an area equal to half of a rectangle with the same base and height. Decompose complex polygons into familiar shapes (rectangles and triangles) and sum their areas.
- Key concepts: triangle area formula, base and height, composing and decomposing polygons.
- Formula/Rule: Area of triangle = (1/2) * base * height = (b * h) / 2.

### MGSE6.G.2
Find the volume of a right rectangular prism with fractional edge lengths by packing it with unit cubes of appropriate unit fraction edge lengths.
- Summary: Apply the volume formula V = l * w * h to rectangular prisms even when dimensions are fractions or mixed numbers, showing that the formula holds identically.
- Key concepts: fractional side lengths, prism volume, cubic units.
- Formula/Rule: V = l * w * h with fractional inputs.

### MGSE6.G.4
Represent three-dimensional figures using nets made up of rectangles and triangles, and use the nets to find the surface area of these figures.
- Summary: Unfold 3D prisms and pyramids into 2D flat nets, find the area of each individual rectangular or triangular face, and add them all together to find total surface area.
- Key concepts: nets, 3D polyhedra, surface area summation.
